## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary: the builder who publishes.** An Oxygen Not Included player who has designed
something worth reusing — a SPOM, a petroleum boiler, a full base layout — and wants it
captured, presented, credited and findable. They arrive from the game with a `.blueprint`
file, or build directly in the browser editor. Authoring tooling is designed first.

**Secondary: the borrower who builds.** A player mid-colony, hunting a proven design to
copy into their own game right now. Discover and blueprint details serve them. This
audience is real and served, but the library is treated as the output of good authoring
tools, not the other way around.

Both are the same person at different moments; the site should let a borrower become a
builder without a mode switch.

## Product Purpose

blueprintnotincluded.org is where ONI blueprints are edited, shared, and accumulated into
collective build knowledge. Success is a growing corpus of blueprints that people can
actually *use* — round-tripped back into the game, not screenshotted — and a library whose
knowledge stays legible as the game and the mod evolve.

The scope is deliberately open-ended: the site will become whatever supports sharing and
editing blueprints and building shared technique.

## Positioning

Four things a wiki, a Steam Workshop post, or a Reddit screenshot cannot do:

1. **Real blueprint data, not pictures.** A blueprint here is structured building data —
   rendered from the game's own assets, analyzed, and reconstructable.
2. **Round-trip with the BlueprintsV2 mod.** `.blueprint` files import and export
   byte-faithfully; the site is the sharing layer for an in-game tool, not a gallery
   beside it.
3. **In-browser editor.** Blueprints can be authored and edited on the site itself.
4. **Derived, searchable metadata.** DLC requirements, rooms, materials, and categories
   are derived from content automatically, so the corpus is filterable rather than a pile
   of posts.

## Operating Context

**Small blueprints are the product; large ones are trophies.** Nobody transplants someone
else's whole base — in this game you take a room or a system. A bathroom, a bedroom, a
SPOM, a petroleum boiler. The site should therefore optimize for sharing small and
medium components and should *not* bend itself to flatter very large blueprints. Large
blueprints are for showing off, and it is acceptable — arguably correct — that they read
as "this thing is gigantic" rather than as something reusable.

**How people actually browse.** Two entry modes: arrive with nothing in mind and browse to
be impressed, or arrive with a specific need ("I need a bathroom"), click into that
category, and hunt for the best blueprint matching their style. Both end in a judgment of
quality and taste, not just a filter match — so the browse surface has to make quality
legible, not merely make results findable.


The user is usually running ONI on the same machine or a second screen. The loop is:
build in game → export via the mod → upload or open on the site → edit/annotate/publish →
another player downloads → imports into their own game. The site is one hop in a workflow
that starts and ends inside the game.

Blueprints carry things that are not buildings: world notes, terrain annotations
(geysers/vents/volcanoes a build is anchored to), dig commands. Content is often
DLC-conditional — a design may require Spaced Out, Frosty, Bionic, or none.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **The one non-negotiable: interoperability with the BlueprintsV2 mod.** Anything the
  site does must survive the round trip into the game. The mod's file format, its
  `metadata` handling, and its coordinate sanitization are hard external contracts. Kevin
  is in continuous contact and cooperation with the mod owner and tracks the mod closely.
- **Everything else is changeable.** No visual, structural, or interaction decision is
  protected for its own sake; anything may be replaced if it improves the editing and
  sharing experience.
- Blueprint rendering uses the game's own extracted assets (OniExtract2024 flat icons);
  those sprites are the content, not decoration.
- **Thumbnails are effectively fixed.** They are generated by a server-side render process
  and currently show the whole design scaled to fit. Changing what a thumbnail depicts
  (crop, scale, framing) is a planned migration with a regeneration pass over the corpus,
  not a design decision a visual pass can make. Treat the current whole-design thumbnail as
  a given constraint; any direction whose central idea requires re-rendering them is
  disqualified until that migration is separately scoped and agreed.
- The editor is a PIXI canvas with a tool/overlay layer.
- Stack is established: Angular 20 + PrimeNG frontend, Express/TypeScript backend,
  MongoDB, shared `lib/`. Not a design decision to reopen.
- Multi-language: English, Chinese, Russian, Korean.
- Accounts, publishing/draft state, forking, versions, comments, ratings, and view/download
  counts already exist.

## Brand Commitments

Name: **blueprintnotincluded.org** (BNI). An unofficial, non-commercial fan project, not
affiliated with Klei. Voice matches the community it serves: practical, player-to-player,
no marketing register. No pricing, no testimonials, no growth claims.

## Evidence on Hand

- The real blueprint corpus in production, with real authors and real usage counts.
- The game's extracted asset database (`database-2024.json`) — every building, element,
  and terrain feature with authentic art.
- The BlueprintsV2 mod itself and a working relationship with its owner.
- **Absent, do not fabricate:** user counts, testimonials, endorsements, any Klei
  affiliation or approval, revenue or pricing of any kind.

## Product Principles

1. **The round trip is sacred.** If a feature cannot survive export back into the game, it
   is either scoped as view-only on purpose or it is not shipped.
2. **Authoring first.** When authoring and browsing conflict, the builder wins; the library
   is downstream of good tools.
3. **Data, not decoration.** Everything shown should be derived from real blueprint
   content. Metadata is computed, never hand-asserted.
4. **The game's art is the content.** The interface frames ONI's own visual language; it
   never competes with it or recolors it.
5. **Nothing is precious except compatibility.** Any part of the site may be rebuilt in
   service of a better editing and sharing experience.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No product-specific standard has been established. Copy and layout must survive
translation into zh/ru/ko without breaking.
